02 / PERSONAL PROJECT

Velocity-Based Trainer.

I designed and built a first-prototype Velocity-Based Trainer (VBT) to deliver the core functionality of commercially available systems at approximately one-quarter of the cost. The project brought mechanical design, CAD, embedded electronics, firmware, and hands-on prototyping together in one working system.

01 / OBJECTIVE

Build useful hardware at a fraction of the cost.

The goal was not to reproduce a commercial product exactly, but to solve the core measurement problem with accessible components and a custom mechanical system.

Objective. Build a VBT for personal use that matches the functionality of commercially available products at ¼ the price.

Commercial VBT systems measure movement velocity by tracking the motion of a tether or similar mechanical interface. I approached the project as a system-integration problem: convert linear tether motion into a measurable rotary signal, process that signal electronically, and present the calculated velocity to the user in real time.

  • Low-cost component selection
  • Compact mechanical packaging
  • Reliable motion measurement
  • Real-time velocity display
02 / SYSTEM DESIGN

Linear motion → rotary measurement.

The mechanical architecture creates a direct measurement path from tether movement to calculated velocity.

The tether winds and unwinds from a custom spool. As the spool rotates, a rotary encoder captures the motion. An ESP32 processes the encoder signal, converts rotational motion into linear displacement, and calculates velocity for an external display.

Motion path: Tether motion → Spool rotation → Rotary encoder → ESP32 → Velocity calculation → External display.

The mechanical design also required consideration of tether routing, spring retraction, packaging, component interfaces, and manufacturability during prototyping.

  • Custom mechanical mechanism
  • Rotary encoder sensing
  • ESP32-based electronics
  • External display integration
  • Calibration and testing
03 / MY CONTRIBUTION

From concept through first prototype.

I owned the development of the prototype and worked across the mechanical and electrical boundaries needed to make the system function as a whole.

Mechanical: Developed the custom mechanism and CAD needed to package the tether, spool, encoder, and supporting components into a functional prototype.

Mechatronics: Integrated the rotary encoder and ESP32-based electronics with the mechanical system and external display.

Firmware & testing: Developed the measurement logic to read encoder speed and display the calculated velocity, then calibrated and tested the prototype to evaluate its performance.

  • CAD design
  • ESP32 firmware development
  • Electromechanical integration
  • Prototyping
  • Calibration and testing
04 / RESULT

A functioning first prototype.

The project resulted in a working system that demonstrates the complete measurement chain from physical motion to a user-facing result.

Successfully developed a first prototype integrating ESP32-based electronics to measure rotary encoder speed and display the calculated velocity on an external display.

Engineering takeaway

This project strengthened my ability to move between mechanical design, electronics, firmware, and testing while keeping the overall system objective in focus.
